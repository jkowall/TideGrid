- **D4. Recently found hosts skip the budget.** A host whose last lookup in this isolate answered `found` within 10 minutes, at most 1,000 hosts, least recently used out first (`guestKnownHosts`), reads the database without a token. The cost: known-host reads under way in one isolate are bounded by 20 times the hosts it has found, not by 20. Only a database `found` answer makes a host known, so a caller cannot raise that count.   - Unverified: whether the 30 s starts at the response or earlier on a client disconnect. If earlier, the cap is optimistic.

### 2026-11-07: GuestGateway keeps found answers for 10 seconds (owner request; coordinator defaults, owner decision 2026-11-08)

The demo build plan's pre-release note for G2.14b-live says `GuestGateway` opens one database connection per call, four per page load, with no rate limit, and asks to "reuse a handle per isolate or cache the public experience for a few seconds, keeping the definite-miss path uncached, before real traffic". The Nov 1 runbook accepted it for owner-only traffic (D9 (a)). The coordinator recommended fixing it before the owner shares `demo.tidegrid.us` with operators. Update, 2026-11-08: the owner confirmed them ([entry](#2026-11-08-owner-confirmation-of-the-coordinator-defaults-owner-decisions)).

- **Cache, not a reused handle.** The coordinator's reading of the Workers I/O model, not checked against Cloudflare's documentation here: a connection opened in one request cannot be used from another, so the gateway caches answers instead. Deployed calls connect through Hyperdrive, which pools to Neon, so the connection an uncached call opens is to Hyperdrive.
- **What is kept.** Only `found` answers, in isolate memory, for 10 seconds, at most 1,000 hosts, least recently used out first. Once one is kept, later calls to the same isolate within 10 seconds open no connection. Calls that arrive before it is kept, or reach another isolate, still read the database; reads in flight are not shared, because a promise settled by another request's I/O is unsupported in Workers. Callers get a copy. The key is the environment and the host as the strict rules parse it, so a host refused in an environment never reaches the cache there. The clock starts before the database read, so a kept answer is never older than 10 seconds from the read's start.
- **What is never kept.** A `miss`, a `withheld` brand, and a database failure. The kill switch and the 503 always come from a fresh read.
- **The cost.** A host disabled, or a brand republished, can keep its old `found` answer in one isolate for up to 10 seconds. The guest Worker serves the old manifest, icons, and service worker for that long, and the kill switch follows within the same bound. Data routes never read the cache.
- **Unsigned, in the isolate.** The [reading](#owner-decision-2026-11-08-g214b-demo-narrowings-coordinator-defaults-2026-10-27) that lets the CORS projection go unsigned covers this cache too: it never leaves the isolate. The CORS projection and this cache use one class (`apps/api-worker/src/hostnames/projection.ts`); the CORS projection's rules are unchanged.
- **The browser proof.** The guest journey's disable case now polls `/sw.js` for up to 20 seconds for the kill switch, since the Worker half can lag the bootstrap's 404 by up to 10 seconds.
- **Not done.** Misses are uncached and unlimited. Today the guest Worker answers only the attached preview names and `demo.tidegrid.us`, so a miss is rare; under Cloudflare for SaaS a fallback origin could send arbitrary hostnames, and a limit on misses belongs to G2.14b-live.
- **Release.** It ships with the next API release, under its own go; nothing was released. Update, 2026-11-08: released with the API in `b1461f3` ([O-REL](#2026-11-08-o-rel-release-of-b1461f3-owner-decisions)).

Canonical: `apps/api-worker/src/hostnames/guest-gateway.ts`, the [API README](../../apps/api-worker/README.md), and the [demo build plan's G2.14b-live note](12-demo-build-plan.md#goal-sequence).

**Fix round of 2026-11-01 (coordinator decisions after the adversarial review of `3da840c`; 0014 was edited in place, as it was not merged).**
- **One instant, and a 30 s bound (decision A).** A ledger row is stamped, and the service and the trigger both decide the limits, at the transaction's time, `date_trunc('milliseconds', now())`. That is the same instant at the same precision, and it is the time the resend's message carries, so the service, the trigger, and the messages read agree at every boundary. The counts take every row committed before the tenant's lock was granted, with no upper bound on its time.
  - A transaction's time is its start. A writer that held its transaction open would carry an old stamp past the 10 minutes and the hour: the review let two resends through 11 ms apart (probe P6).
  - So a resend whose transaction has run longer than 30 s is refused `booking_email_resends_late`, at its ledger insert and again at commit. A transaction may shorten the bound with `app.resend_max_transaction`, never lengthen it. A malformed or negative value closes the bound.
  - A stamp therefore lags its commit by at most 30 s, and that is the slack. Measured by commit times, a booking's resend may commit as little as 9 minutes 30 seconds after its previous one, and 51 of the operator's resends may commit within 59 minutes 30 seconds.
  - Those figures hold for the service and for any writer that does none of the following. The commit check bounds the stamp only up to the moment its deferred event runs; any wait after that, before the commit record, is unbounded. That is a class, not one escape (final check of 2026-11-01): a deferred foreign key whose referenced row another session holds `FOR UPDATE` (such as `waiver_requests_superseded_by_fkey` or `booking_cancellations_refund`; the check's probe let two resends commit 45 ms apart), `PREPARE TRANSACTION` where `max_prepared_transactions` is above 0, and `SET CONSTRAINTS ... IMMEDIATE`. Each needs a writer with `tidegrid_app`'s credentials that bypasses the service, the same threat class as before; the service does none of them. The bound relies on `max_prepared_transactions` being 0, which the catalog suite checks; no migration asserts it. Bounding sends at the delivery claim instead is a possible pre-pilot follow-up (proposed).
  - A clock stamp taken after the lock was tried first and dropped. It moved the ledger time off its message's time, which broke the API's availability read, and a writer could still hold its transaction open after the stamp.
- **A late resend is answered 503 `booking_email_busy`.** The transaction rolls back whole: no message is queued, so delivery stays at most once; no ledger or audit row is written, and nothing is stored for the key. The same key may be sent again. The console gives up on a call after 15 s, so when the 30 s bound is the cause staff see that the console can't reach TideGrid, not this answer; that failure is not final, and the dialog keeps the same key for Send again.
  - The route maps the error and documents the 503; `openapi.json` is regenerated for that alone. This is a coordinator-authorized change under `apps/api-worker`.
  - Measured locally: a service resend runs about 20 ms, and the 20th of 20 at once on one tenant about 450 ms, queued on the tenant's lock. A delivery claim it waits for is a short transaction; the provider call is outside it. Reaching 30 s would take over 100 resends of one tenant at once at Neon's round trips, so the bound stays at 30 s.
- **A lock at the message.** Every `booking.resend:` message insert takes the tenant's resend lock before its key is claimed. For the service this is a no-op. A writer that bypasses the service now waits for a service resend in progress while holding no resend key. Before, its claimed key and the service's held lock deadlocked, and the staff request was aborted (probe P1).
- **Invoker, pinned.** The catalog suite pins the five 0014 functions as SECURITY INVOKER and fixes their volatility. The runtime may execute only `app.booking_resend_facts` and `app.booking_resend_max_transaction`.

## V1 scope replaced by V2

The V2 package replaces these V1 directions:

- broad departure operations as the primary product;
- the Dock, Growth, Fleet, and Enterprise pricing schedule;
- a 0.75% to 3% fee limited to TideGrid-processed payments;
- bundled guided onboarding that is waived with annual purchase;
- native offline captain operations in the initial roadmap;
- manifests, boarding, vessel readiness, crew management, medical review, incident management, and serialized equipment in MVP;
- packages, gift cards, points, and several stored-value types at launch;
- Terminal, POS, live OTA synchronization, waitlists, and broad accounting integrations in the pilot.

V1 database tables, API paths, diagrams, tests, and traceability entries describe the historical design. They are not implementation instructions for V2.

## Contracts to define after paid validation

Before production implementation, create or revise contracts for:

- brand configuration, custom hostnames, native app identity, and app releases;
- product, scheduled trip, boat, capacity hold, booking, participant, and order lines;
- deposits, payment attempts, external payments, refunds, service credits, tips, and platform-fee assessments;
- waiver template, immutable version, requirement, signature, guardian relationship, and rendered evidence;
- consent, message, delivery, booking-scoped reply, and provider callback;
- pooled equipment, holds, participant allocations, and manual blocks;
- weather evidence, trip changes, affected-booking snapshots, and remedies;
- package accounts, unit holds, redemption, release, restoration, and import entries.

The V2 architecture document defines behavioral boundaries. The current schema and OpenAPI file remain V1 until this contract work passes review.

## Decision process

Record a dated V2 decision when new evidence changes product scope, price, fee treatment, or an architecture invariant. Update the affected V2 documents in the same change.

Create a new ADR before implementation changes a retained technical decision. Do not edit a V1 ADR to make history appear consistent with V2.
