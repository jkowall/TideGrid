# TideGrid pre-customer formation and validation plan

**Status:** Canonical near-term execution sequence; no filing, spending, outreach, or publication is authorized by this document

## Recommendation

Create and approve the customer discussion materials first. Then form a bare-bones, single-member Florida LLC before contacting operators. Keep the expensive legal, insurance, payment, messaging, native-store, and production-readiness work behind evidence from the customer interviews.

Formation and paid-pilot readiness are separate gates:

1. **Bare-bones formation** creates the legal entity, EIN, basic company records, bank account, domain email, and compliance calendar.
2. **Paid-pilot readiness** adds customer contracts, privacy and messaging review, insurance, Stripe and provider readiness, production security and continuity, and the funded build plan.

Creating the LLC does not validate the product, authorize the production build, or make the current prices safe to quote as final.

## Ordered plan

### Step 1: Approve the validation materials

Before filing or contacting anyone:

- review the customer-facing [TideGrid validation brief](../customer/tidegrid-validation-brief.md);
- review the internal [operator validation guide](../customer/operator-validation-guide.md);
- remove claims that cannot be demonstrated or clearly label them as proposed;
- decide whether the working name `TideGrid` is acceptable for the next name-clearance step; and
- create a private prospect list with no customer information committed to this public repository.

The brief is a discussion aid, not a pitch deck or binding offer. During an interview, ask about the operator's last real booking, change, refund, waiver, weather disruption, and current costs before showing the concept or price.

**Exit:** The brief, interview guide, current V2 scope, and pricing worksheet describe the same product and fee base.

### Step 2: Resolve the filing inputs privately

Do not put home addresses, taxpayer identifiers, signatures, recovery codes, or identity documents in this repository. Record the final answers in a private filing packet:

| Filing decision | Bare-bones recommendation |
|---|---|
| Legal name | `TideGrid LLC` only if the current Sunbiz search and initial trademark screen are clear enough to proceed |
| Entity | Florida limited liability company |
| Ownership | One member with a 100% interest |
| Management | Member-managed |
| Principal and mailing address | Use a legitimate address the owner accepts will appear in public records; a registered-agent address is not automatically a business address |
| Registered agent | Self-agent for lowest cash cost only if public-address exposure and reliable availability are acceptable; otherwise use a commercial Florida agent |
| Purpose | Omit the optional purpose field unless counsel identifies a reason to include it |
| Manager or authorized representative | Do not list the member as `MGR`; add an authorized representative only when needed for filing, banking, or another documented purpose |
| Effective date | Use the actual intended start date; do not delay effectiveness if customer outreach will start immediately |
| Correspondence email | Use a company-controlled address because Florida filing correspondence and addresses may become public records |
| Optional state documents | Skip the $5 certificate of status and $30 certified copy unless a bank or other provider requires one |

Perform these screens immediately before filing:

1. Search the exact and similar legal names in [Sunbiz](https://dos.fl.gov/sunbiz/search/).
2. Search the working product name and close variants in the [USPTO trademark database](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search).
3. Check the intended domains through an accredited registrar or [ICANN Lookup](https://lookup.icann.org/).
4. Review current and former employment obligations before assigning any pre-formation work to the LLC.

A Sunbiz result is not trademark clearance, and a domain registration creates no trademark right. The current plan does not claim that `TideGrid` is available.

**Exit:** Every required filing field is decided, public exposure is understood, and the owner has accepted the recurring compliance obligation.

### Step 3: File the Florida LLC

1. File directly through the [Florida Division of Corporations](https://dos.fl.gov/sunbiz/start-business/efile/fl-llc/).
2. Pay the required $100 filing fee and $25 registered-agent designation fee.
3. Decline optional copies unless they are needed.
4. Save the submitted filing, payment receipt, and state acknowledgment in company-controlled private storage.
5. Verify the entity appears as active in Sunbiz and confirm the exact legal name, document number, effective date, addresses, agent, and correspondence email.
6. Calendar the first annual report for January 1 through May 1 of the following calendar year. The current published fee is $138.75, rising to $538.75 after May 1.

The minimum state filing is $125. A commercial registered agent, local receipts, domain, email, or bank requirements are separate costs.

**Exit:** Sunbiz shows the single-member Florida LLC as active and the filed details match the approved private packet.

### Step 4: Complete the bare-bones company setup

Complete these items before operator outreach:

1. Execute a single-member operating agreement and initial written action.
2. Record a one-line membership ledger showing the owner's 100% interest.
3. Assign identified pre-formation TideGrid work to the LLC with a written schedule, while excluding third-party property and recording applicable licenses.
4. Obtain the EIN directly from the [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number) at no charge after the state filing is accepted.
5. Open a company bank account, fund the recorded initial contribution, and keep company and personal spending separate.
6. Start a simple bookkeeping ledger and calendar the state annual report, federal tax review, registered-agent renewal, domains, email, and local receipts.
7. Create a company-controlled domain email for outreach. Do not represent that the product is available or that a pilot is approved.
8. If the business operates from Pompano Beach, confirm zoning and Business Tax Receipt requirements with the city, then obtain the city receipt before the Broward County receipt when required. Do not assume a home business is exempt.
9. Recheck the current [FinCEN BOI page](https://www.fincen.gov/boi) and record the conclusion. As of this plan's date, US-created companies are exempt, but the rule must be checked again on the filing date.

A CPA review can remain narrow at this stage: confirm the default single-member tax treatment, W-9 approach, estimated-tax calendar, and bookkeeping setup. Do not elect S-corporation treatment automatically.

**Exit:** The entity, EIN, company records, bank separation, company email, compliance calendar, and required local-registration decision are documented.

### Step 5: Run the 12-interview validation

Use three declared tranches so wording can improve and the last interviews can correct recruiting gaps without moving the success criteria:

Recruit six dive operators and two each from fishing, sightseeing, and private-charter businesses. This keeps dive as the beachhead while testing whether the shared product applies to adjacent operators.

#### Interviews 1 to 3: Calibrate the interview

- Start with recent behavior and current systems.
- Quantify booking volume, direct and OTA mix, repeat customers, software and payment cost, staff time, exceptions, and switching constraints.
- Show the concept brief only after the operator's current workflow is understood.
- Adjust only question wording and timing when the guide fails to produce specific events or usable economic data.

#### Interviews 4 to 9: Use the stabilized guide

- Use the same dated concept version and standard price structure.
- Walk through the proposed workflow and boundaries only after past behavior and problem ranking.
- Ask for a concrete next step, such as sharing a sanitized aggregate report, scheduling a data-readiness review, or scheduling a separate commercial-validation meeting.
- Record objections and missing context without promising a feature or changing scope in response to one request.

#### Interviews 10 to 12: Fill declared evidence gaps

- After interview 9, identify missing operator types and qualification ranges.
- Recruit the final three interviews to fill those gaps, not to manufacture a favorable result.
- Use the stabilized guide and concept without changing the thresholds.

Evaluate the gate only after all 12 are complete. Do not change the denominator or discard negative evidence.

#### Qualified-prospect feasibility and commercial follow-up

Authorize the bounded feasibility and cost spikes in the roadmap only when at least five of the 12 operators meet the direct-first profile and at least three provisionally Native-qualified prospects across two operator types accept a commercial follow-up. Then hold that separate meeting with each qualified Native prospect. Use the verified aggregate economics, disclose the full standard price before the pilot credit, and ask the authorized buyer for a written conditional commitment only after confirming the standard scope, fee base, pass-throughs, and store-account responsibilities.

Across all 12 interviews, preserve the thresholds defined once in the roadmap [validation gate](05-roadmap-validation.md#validation-gate); do not move them in response to any single interview.

Compliments, future-tense interest, feature requests, and willingness to join a mailing list do not count as demand evidence.

**Exit:** Twelve structured records exist, including disconfirming evidence. When both the five-of-12 gate and the three-provisional-Native-prospect gate pass, the feasibility evidence and qualified commercial follow-up outcomes also exist. The owner records a go, change, or stop decision.

### Step 6: Fund and complete paid-pilot readiness

Only after the interview thresholds of the [Validation gate](05-roadmap-validation.md#validation-gate) support continuing:

- approve the bottom-up build and delivery budget, funding source, contingency, and refund or long-stop treatment;
- obtain counsel-approved pilot contracts, privacy and guest terms, waiver-process allocation, payment responsibilities, SMS terms, support terms, and native-store responsibilities;
- bind the required insurance;
- complete accounting, tax, local-registration, security, continuity, domain, public legal-page, and provider-account readiness;
- verify each pilot operator's entity, D-U-N-S record, organization developer accounts, domain, approved content, and Stripe configuration; and
- sign all three recurring agreements and collect all three settled $2,250 Pilot Native setup payments before starting the full production build.

The itemized live-customer-readiness estimate remains $6,405 to $17,100, with $8,000 to $18,000 retained as a conservative authorization ceiling until quotes replace assumptions. That amount is not the price of filing the LLC.

## Immediate owner decisions

The filing cannot proceed until the owner chooses:

1. whether to attempt `TideGrid LLC` after live name and trademark screening;
2. the public principal and mailing addresses;
3. self-agent or commercial registered agent;
4. the company correspondence email;
5. the effective date and initial contribution; and
6. whether to complete Pompano Beach and Broward registrations before the first validation outreach or after the city confirms when they are required.

These decisions contain private information and should be handled outside the public repository.

## Stop conditions

Stop before filing if the legal name is not acceptable, the owner has not accepted the public-address implications, or the annual compliance obligation is not worth taking on before validation.

Stop before outreach if the materials overstate product availability, contain an unsupported customer claim, or present proposed pricing as final.

Stop before accepting money, production data, or a binding pilot agreement until the paid-pilot readiness gate passes. Bare-bones formation does not satisfy that gate.
