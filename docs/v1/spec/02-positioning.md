# 2. Product positioning and differentiation

## Position

**Category:** Passenger-vessel operations platform  
**Core promise:** Every departure, under control.  
**Positioning statement:** TideGrid gives passenger-vessel operators one connected operational record for each departure, from bookable capacity through back-ashore closeout.

## Competitive reality

FareHarbor, Peek Pro, Xola, and Bókun already market booking, manifests, staff assignment, POS, packages, communications, analytics, offline or mobile check-in, and OTA connectivity. TideGrid cannot claim differentiation merely by implementing those category requirements.

| Category norm | TideGrid differentiation |
|---|---|
| Booking calendar and checkout | Generalized requirements across vessels, crew, docks, permits, equipment, and capacity dimensions |
| Manifest and mobile check-in | Immutable departure snapshot plus offline execution, return-ashore confirmation, incidents, and closeout |
| Staff assignment | Qualification, availability, role, credential expiration, ratio rules, and documented overrides |
| Inventory | Serialized and pooled time-bound equipment integrated with checkout and participant allocation |
| Weather messaging | Human-approved disruption case that coordinates resources, partial-party movement, credit, refund, and rebooking |
| Packages and gift cards | Unit-aware immutable ledgers with concurrent redemption protection |
| Reporting | Departure-centered operational and financial reconciliation |
| Generic vertical fields | Shared maritime core plus explicit dive, fishing, and excursion modules |

## Target segments

1. Dive operators with finite tanks and rental equipment, certification and medical workflows, repeat customers, and offshore execution.
2. Multi-vessel excursion and sightseeing operators with crew, dock, capacity, weather, and dispatch complexity.
3. Fishing charter operators combining private or shared inventory, guides, vessels, equipment, and trip closeout.
4. Private-charter businesses requiring inquiry, option hold, quote, acceptance, and change-order workflows.

The first beachhead should favor operators with at least two of these pains: multiple vessels, frequent weather changes, finite equipment, repeat package customers, multiple departures per day, or unreliable dock connectivity.

## Buying criteria

- Demonstrable prevention of vessel, captain, seat, package, and equipment conflicts.
- Fast migration from a generic booking platform.
- Direct ownership of customer and operational data.
- Clear, predictable subscription, fleet entitlements, and declining platform-fee rates as operators scale.
- Reliable dockside and offline workflows.
- Stripe reconciliation that an accountant can trace.
- Practical support for a one-location operator without blocking fleet growth.

## Messaging boundaries

TideGrid coordinates information and workflows. It does not navigate a vessel, certify seaworthiness, replace a captain’s authority, make medical determinations, guarantee regulatory compliance, or automatically decide whether a departure is safe.

## Go-to-market proof sequence

1. Demonstrate one departure from availability through closeout.
2. Show a concurrent final-seat or final-package-unit race resolving safely.
3. Put the captain client offline, complete boarding and return commands, then synchronize.
4. Run a weather cancellation with partial-party rebooking and refunds.
5. Reconcile the charge, snapshotted plan application fee, refund, tip, and payout.
