## 1. Executive brand recommendation

### Decision

Build the brand around **departure control**, not online booking. Passenger-vessel operators already see many products promising more bookings, easier scheduling, and revenue growth. Tidegrid should own the operating work that starts when capacity goes on sale and ends after the vessel returns, the trip closes, and the money reconciles.

Use:

- **Public category:** Passenger-vessel operations platform
- **Explanatory category line:** Departure operations software for passenger-vessel operators
- **Core position:** One connected operational record for each departure
- **Brand promise and tagline:** Every departure, under control.
- **Homepage lead:** Run every departure from booking to back ashore.
- **Selected identity:** Tideline Grid, using the existing mark, palette, and typography
- **Commercial proof:** Flat monthly subscription plus a 1% Tidegrid platform fee, with Stripe processing disclosed separately

The phrase “operating system” can explain the ambition in founder, investor, and long-form product material. Use “operations platform” in first-touch customer copy. Competitors already use operating-system language, and the term can imply broader control over navigation, safety, maintenance, or vessel systems than Tidegrid provides.

### Strategic thesis

Operators do not experience bookings, waivers, crew, gear, payments, and weather changes as separate jobs. Each item either clears or blocks a departure. Tidegrid should organize the product and the story around that unit of work.

The strongest message is concrete:

> A departure has a vessel, capacity, crew, guests, documents, equipment, money, and a readiness state. Tidegrid keeps that record connected from sale through closeout.

This position supports a one-boat operator because it reduces dockside coordination. It supports a fleet because the same model exposes conflicts, readiness, and performance across locations.

### Competitive field and open territory

| Competitor | Current emphasis | Strength | Gap Tidegrid can own |
|---|---|---|---|
| [FareHarbor](https://fareharbor.com/) | All-in-one booking, selling, support, and scale across tours and activities | Scale, support, checkout, broad ecosystem | Maritime readiness and departure execution are not its lead story |
| [Peek Pro](https://www.peekpro.com/solutions/tour-operator-software) | Revenue growth, dynamic pricing, mobile manifests, POS, and guest self-service | Conversion and revenue tooling | Crew, vessel, credential, equipment, incident, and closeout depth lack a single departure-control narrative |
| [Xola](https://www.xola.com/tour-management-software) | Unified booking and marketing software with schedules, guides, resources, communication, and analytics | Strong overlap in tour operations; a boat-tour page speaks to fleet and roster management | Tidegrid must prove deeper vessel-specific workflows and offline trip execution |
| [Bókun](https://www.bokun.io/pricing) | Distribution, Viator access, booking management, and tiered subscription-plus-fee pricing | Channel reach and transparent published tiers | Departure readiness and offshore execution sit outside its central value story |
| [Indexic](https://www.indexic.net/boat-charter-software) | Boat and rental booking, fleet resources, personnel, waivers, rescheduling, POS, and reporting | Direct marine relevance and broad operational features | Tidegrid can offer a calmer system centered on one departure record rather than a suite of separately named tools |
| [GoFish.Rocks](https://www.gofish.rocks/) | Marine-native booking, manifests, waitlists, communication, migration, and a flat per-passenger fee | Operator credibility and pricing clarity | Tidegrid can differentiate through crew credentials, equipment, readiness, offline execution, incident capture, and closeout |

Competitors already claim “all-in-one,” “built for boats,” resource management, manifests, and transparent pricing. Tidegrid needs product proof for the complete departure record. Maritime vocabulary and a green logo cannot carry the position by themselves.

### Positioning options considered

| Territory | Advantage | Weakness | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Better boat booking | Easy to understand | Crowded and undersells the product | Reject |
| Marine-native software | Credible vertical focus | Indexic, GoFish, Xola, and others already make similar claims | Use as proof |
| Departure control | Connects booking, readiness, execution, and closeout | Requires a short explanation on first exposure | **Adopt** |
| Reliable offshore operations | Highlights offline capability | Sounds like a safety or connectivity guarantee | Use as substantiated product proof |
| Operator-friendly economics | Creates a sharp buying argument | Pricing can be copied and does not explain the workflow | Use in conversion copy |

### Launch priorities

1. Clear or replace the working name before further brand investment.
2. Validate “departure” as the organizing concept with operators.
3. Build a demo around one departure from booking through closeout.
4. Prove three hard differentiators: offline execution, readiness dependencies, and connected reconciliation.
5. Publish pricing only after the monthly subscription amount and Stripe fee disclosure are final.

## 2. Final positioning

### One-sentence positioning statement

**Tidegrid is the passenger-vessel operations platform that keeps each departure crewed, equipped, documented, boarded, completed, and reconciled in one connected record.**

### Short elevator pitch

Tidegrid helps dive boats, fishing charters, cruises, and passenger-vessel fleets run the work behind each trip. It connects booking, waivers, crew, vessels, gear, check-in, weather changes, payments, and closeout, with dockside and offline workflows built for the way operators work.

### Longer company description

Tidegrid is departure operations software for passenger-vessel businesses. Operators use it to sell seats and private charters, coordinate vessels and crew, manage passenger documents and finite equipment, check guests in, handle weather changes, run offline-capable trip workflows, and close each departure with payments, tips, incidents, and reconciliation in one record.

Tidegrid serves owner-operators and multi-location fleets across diving, fishing, sightseeing, snorkeling, sunset cruises, and private charters. Its flat monthly subscription and 1% platform fee give operators a direct alternative to generic tour systems whose cost and complexity can grow faster than the value they deliver.

### Five supporting value propositions

1. **See readiness before the dock:** Find the missing crew assignment, document, waiver, payment, or equipment allocation while the team can still act.
2. **Protect finite operating capacity:** Sell shared seats and private charters against the vessels, people, docks, gear, and limits required to run them.
3. **Keep field work available:** Give captains and dock teams assigned manifests, boarding, checks, incidents, and queued changes with explicit offline state.
4. **Carry disruptions through the operation:** Connect the weather decision to messages, credits, refunds, rebooking, capacity, and standby offers.
5. **Close the operating and financial record together:** Join trip state, dockside point of sale, tips, incidents, credits, and reconciliation around the departure that produced them.

### Category sentence by context

| Context | Language |
|---|---|
| Homepage eyebrow | Departure operations for passenger-vessel operators |
| Sales introduction | A passenger-vessel operations platform |
| Search and comparison pages | Boat booking and departure operations software |
| Investor or partner description | The departure operating system for passenger-vessel operators |
| App-store subtitle | Vessel departures, crew, guests, and gear |

### Positioning boundaries

Tidegrid coordinates operational records and workflows. It does not navigate a vessel, replace the captain’s authority, make weather decisions, certify seaworthiness, guarantee regulatory compliance, or serve as the official substitute for records that law or an operator’s procedures require elsewhere. Customer copy must keep these boundaries clear.

## 3. Recommended category language

### Recommendation

Use **passenger-vessel operations platform** as the category and **departure operations software** as the plain-language explanation.

“Passenger-vessel” filters out freight, marina, recreational lifestyle, and tide-prediction interpretations. “Operations” creates room for booking, readiness, execution, and closeout. “Platform” signals breadth without the overclaim carried by “operating system.”

### Category hierarchy

1. **Passenger-vessel operations platform**
2. **Departure operations software**
3. **Boat booking, manifests, crew, gear, payments, and closeout**
4. Segment modifiers such as “for dive operators” or “for fishing charters”

### Search-friendly descriptors

Use these in page titles, metadata, comparison pages, and paid search:

- Boat booking and operations software
- Passenger-vessel booking software
- Dive boat operations software
- Fishing charter booking and manifest software
- Boat crew and equipment scheduling
- Offline passenger manifest and check-in
- Weather rebooking software for boat operators

Avoid “marine management software” as the lead category. It spans shipping, marinas, maintenance, and vessel tracking. Avoid “tour operating system” as the first phrase because Peek and other tour platforms use similar language.

## 4. Brand promise, mission, and vision

### Brand promise

**Every departure, under control.**

Use this as a standard of operational clarity, not as a guarantee that Tidegrid controls weather, safety, or trip outcomes. Pair it with the category line on first exposure.

### Mission

**Give passenger-vessel operators one dependable system to prepare, run, and close each trip.**

### Vision

**Passenger-vessel teams start each departure with the people, resources, records, and decisions they need in one place.**

### Brand principles

- **Prepared:** Show missing work before it reaches the dock.
- **Clear:** Give each role the next decision and the reason behind it.
- **Calm:** Use measured language, stable layouts, and explicit status.
- **Practical:** Match dockside work instead of forcing a generic tour model.
- **Accountable:** Preserve who changed what, when, and why.
- **Focused:** Keep the departure at the center of the system.

## 5. Tagline recommendation

### Primary tagline

**Every departure, under control.**

It names the operating unit, delivers the emotional outcome, and fits the selected grid-and-tide mark. The line needs an explanatory category phrase until the brand has recognition.

### Alternative taglines

1. **From booked to back ashore.** Best lifecycle alternative.
2. **One system for every departure.** Best direct product alternative.
3. **Your operation, ready to get underway.** Best readiness alternative, but less distinctive.

### Candidate-line evaluation

| Candidate | Communicates well | Misses or weakens | Risk | Best role | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Every departure, under control.** | Outcome, calm, authority, departure focus | Does not name the audience or software category | “Control” can sound absolute or safety-related | Tagline and promise | **Adopt; pair with category language** |
| **Run the whole boat, not just the booking.** | Breadth beyond checkout | “Whole boat” implies navigation, maintenance, or safety systems | Overclaim and formulaic contrast | Challenger campaign | Revise to “Run the operation behind the trip.” |

| **Your vessels, crew, guests, and gear in sync.** | Concrete dependencies and target context | Long; “sync” sounds like generic software | Implies perfect data synchronization | Supporting message | Use beneath feature or workflow copy |

### Campaign lines

- **From booked to back ashore.**
- **Run the operation behind the trip.**
- **When weather changes the plan, move the work with it.**

### Usage rule

Place the tagline near the wordmark only when the category appears in the same view. A favicon, app icon, uniform mark, or printed manifest should use the mark without the tagline.

## 6. Messaging hierarchy

### Master hierarchy

| Level | Message | Evidence the product must show |
|---|---|---|
| Category | Passenger-vessel operations platform | Vessel, crew, guest, gear, and departure models in the interface |
| Promise | Every departure, under control. | Readiness state, conflicts, ownership, timestamps, and closeout |
| Primary value | Run each trip from booking to back ashore in one connected record | A complete departure timeline |
| Pillar 1 | Know what is ready and what can block departure | Resource conflicts, missing waivers, expired credentials, maintenance blocks |
| Pillar 2 | Keep the dock moving when time and connectivity are tight | Fast check-in, offline manifests, queued updates, large touch targets |
| Pillar 3 | Move weather changes without rebuilding the day by hand | Bulk communication, credits, self-service rebooking, waitlist offers |
| Pillar 4 | Close the trip with the same record used to run it | Incidents, tips, payments, refunds, reconciliation, reports |
| Commercial proof | Flat monthly subscription plus a 1% Tidegrid platform fee | Published terms and a fee example with Stripe processing separate |

### Owners and general managers

- **Lead:** See how the whole operation is running before a missed document, resource conflict, or weather call reaches the dock.
- **Value:** More usable capacity, less fragmented software, cleaner financial closeout.
- **Proof:** Fleet readiness view, utilization, trip-card liability, payment reconciliation, audit history.
- **CTA:** See Tidegrid across a full operating day.

### Operations staff and dispatchers

- **Lead:** Build the departure once and keep every dependency attached to it.
- **Value:** Fewer double bookings, fewer last-minute calls, faster changes.
- **Proof:** Shared resource calendar, warnings, bulk actions, rebooking, waitlists.
- **CTA:** Walk through a weather-change workflow.

### Captains and trip leaders

- **Lead:** Get the manifest, readiness checks, boarding state, and trip record in one field-ready view.
- **Value:** Less searching and re-entry at the point of departure.
- **Proof:** Offline state, last-sync time, queued changes, role-specific checklist, incident record.
- **CTA:** Try the departure view on a phone.

### Customers and trip participants

- **Lead:** Complete the required steps before arrival and handle changes without a phone queue.
- **Value:** Faster check-in, clear instructions, direct rebooking choices.
- **Proof:** Mobile waiver, customer portal, reminders, credits, rebooking, standby offer.
- **CTA:** Review your trip.

### Accountants and finance staff

- **Lead:** Reconcile what sold, what ran, what changed, and how tips and credits moved.
- **Value:** A traceable closeout record with fewer spreadsheet joins.
- **Proof:** Payment state, refunds, credits, tips, trip-card balances, exports, audit trail.
- **CTA:** Review a completed departure.

## 7. Audience-specific positioning

### Dive boat operators

- **Primary pain:** Bookings, certifications, medical forms, waivers, tanks, rental gear, crew, and manifests live in separate systems.
- **Promise:** Get each diver, crew member, vessel, and equipment allocation ready before boarding.
- **Supporting capabilities:** Certification and medical workflows; finite rental equipment; offline manifest and check-in.
- **Headline:** **A dive operation built around the departure.**
- **Proof point:** Show one diver record linked to waiver, certification, gear allocation, boarding state, and trip-card use.
- **Primary objection:** “Dive workflows are too specific for a new booking system.”
- **Response:** Demonstrate the certification, medical, equipment, divemaster, and trip-card workflow before showing checkout. Avoid claiming standards support until the product implements named agency and operator rules.

### Fishing charter operators

- **Primary pain:** Shared-seat trips, private charters, weather calls, walk-ups, deposits, tips, and captain availability create daily manual work.
- **Promise:** Run open-party and private trips from the same vessel-aware schedule.
- **Supporting capabilities:** Per-seat and whole-boat inventory; weather rebooking and standby offers; captain scheduling and tip allocation.
- **Headline:** **Fill the trip. Keep the boat and captain clear.**
- **Proof point:** Show a weather-cancelled trip moved into credits and self-service rebooking without overselling the replacement departure.
- **Primary objection:** “A flat per-passenger tool costs less.”
- **Response:** Compare total cost with the value of crew, credential, equipment, readiness, offline, and closeout workflows. Do not compete on fee percentage alone.

### Sunset and sightseeing cruise operators

- **Primary pain:** High-volume check-in, mixed sales channels, late arrivals, private events, and quick turnarounds strain front-desk and dock teams.
- **Promise:** Board on time with one live view of capacity, payments, waivers, and passenger state.
- **Supporting capabilities:** Fast check-in and boarding; POS and channel-connected capacity; manifest and departure readiness.
- **Headline:** **Keep the line moving and the departure on time.**
- **Proof point:** Measure median check-in time and unresolved passenger exceptions ten minutes before departure.
- **Primary objection:** “Our current ticketing system already handles volume.”
- **Response:** Lead with the work around the ticket: crew, vessel readiness, waivers, exceptions, offline access, and closeout. If the incumbent already handles those jobs, Tidegrid needs a migration or economics advantage to win.

### Private charter operators

- **Primary pain:** Quotes, deposits, custom requests, vessel and crew availability, contracts, guest details, and final balances drift across email and spreadsheets.
- **Promise:** Keep each charter’s commercial and operating details attached from inquiry through closeout.
- **Supporting capabilities:** Whole-boat availability; payment schedule and credits; guest, crew, vessel, and request record.
- **Headline:** **One charter record from deposit to return.**
- **Proof point:** Show a custom charter moving from hold to confirmed departure without a vessel or captain conflict.
- **Primary objection:** “Our charters are too custom for a system.”
- **Response:** Show configurable requirements and exceptions while protecting the common departure record. Do not force private charters into a per-seat model.

### Multi-vessel and multi-location operators

- **Primary pain:** Local teams make schedule and readiness decisions without a shared fleet view or consistent closeout process.
- **Promise:** Give each location control of the day while management sees conflicts, readiness, and performance across the fleet.
- **Supporting capabilities:** Cross-location resources and permissions; credential and document expiry; standardized reporting and reconciliation.
- **Headline:** **One operating view across every vessel and location.**
- **Proof point:** Show a fleet dashboard that drills into the owner, cause, and resolution of each blocked departure.
- **Primary objection:** “A focused platform will not meet enterprise controls.”
- **Response:** Prove permissions, audit history, exports, integration boundaries, support model, and data migration. Avoid the word “enterprise” until those controls exist.

## 8. Verbal identity

### Name explanation

“Tide” grounds the brand in the maritime environment, timing, movement, and changing conditions. “Grid” describes the schedules, resources, capacity, and dependencies that an operator coordinates. The name says that natural movement passes through an organized operating structure, which the selected mark depicts with horizontal tide bands crossing vertical grid lines.

This explanation helps comprehension, but it does not solve the exact-name conflict identified in the diligence section.

### Tone of voice

| Trait | Write like this | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Prepared | Name the condition, owner, and next action | Vague reassurance |
| Clear | “Captain credential expires in 12 days.” | “There may be an issue with this resource.” |
| Calm | “Weather review required by 6:00 AM.” | “Storm alert! Act now!” |
| Decisive | “Block departure” or “Approve exception” | “You might want to consider…” |
| Practical | Use vessel, crew, guest, gear, waiver, and departure | Transformation jargon |
| Trustworthy | State product state and limits | Safety, compliance, uptime, or revenue guarantees |

### Writing rules

- Put the departure, operator, or customer at the start of the sentence.
- Use verbs tied to work: assign, board, block, move, refund, reconcile, close.
- Name the object and status. “Waiver missing” beats “Incomplete item.”
- Keep warnings factual. Explain the consequence and action.
- Use maritime terms that operators know. Explain terms shown to customers.
- Reserve humor for low-risk marketing. Keep product and support language steady.
- Use sentence case in the interface. Avoid all-caps alerts.
- Do not use pirate language, luxury-yacht cues, battle language, or generic productivity slogans.

### Preferred and avoided language

| Preferred | Avoid | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Departure readiness | Seamless operations | Names the job |
| Vessel conflict | Scheduling hiccup | States the issue |
| Changes queued; last synced 9:42 AM | You are offline | Shows consequence and state |
| Captain assignment required | Resource unavailable | Names the role |
| Weather decision pending | Bad weather | Preserves operator authority |
| Passenger manifest | Attendee list | Fits the operating context |
| Move guests to another departure | Optimize disruption workflows | Uses a direct action |
| Close departure | Complete journey lifecycle | Fits the task |
| Built for passenger-vessel operators | Purpose-built for your unique needs | Names the market |
| 1% Tidegrid platform fee | Low fees | Gives the number |

### Customer-facing terminology

| Concept | Customer language | Operator language | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commercial record | Booking | Booking | Use “reservation” only in legal or partner contexts that require it |
| Scheduled operating instance | Trip and departure time | Departure | Use “departure” in statuses, manifests, and resource planning |
| Person attending | Guest | Passenger; diver or angler where relevant | Use “passenger” for manifests and capacity; avoid “pax” in customer copy |
| Person covered by a booking | Guest | Participant when forms apply to each individual | Do not make “participant” the main customer noun |
| Staff assigned to a departure | Crew | Crew, then role such as captain or divemaster | Use specific roles in actions and warnings |
| Change caused by weather or operations | Trip update | Disruption or service change | Tell customers the outcome: delayed, moved, cancelled, or ready to rebook |
| Product sold on a schedule | Experience or trip | Offering | Avoid “product” in customer copy |
| Readiness issue | Action needed | Warning or blocker | Reserve “blocked” for a rule that prevents departure readiness |

### Product status language

- **Ready:** Required checks have passed.
- **Needs attention:** Work remains, but the current rules do not block the departure.
- **Blocked:** A required item prevents readiness.
- **Pending decision:** A named operator must decide.
- **Changes queued:** The device saved work that has not synced.
- **Conflict:** The same finite resource has incompatible assignments.
- **Closed:** The trip returned and required closeout steps are complete.

## 9. Product naming architecture

### Recommendation

Sell **one Tidegrid platform**. Use plain feature-area names in navigation. Name a capability only when it has a distinct delivery surface, user context, or integration contract.

The proposed Book, Ops, Crew, Gear, and Pay module names create artificial purchase boundaries inside one connected departure record. Operators should not need to learn a portfolio before they understand the product.

### Customer-facing architecture

| Level | Recommended name | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Company and platform | **Tidegrid** | The subscription and master product |
| Mobile and offline operating surface | **Tidegrid Deck** | Captain, crew, manifest, boarding, readiness, incident, and closeout work |
| Integration surface | **Tidegrid Connect** | APIs, webhooks, widgets, partner connections, and developer material |
| Feature areas | **Bookings, Departures, Crew, Gear, Payments, Reports** | Product navigation and sales capability groups |

### Starting-name decisions

| Starting name | Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Tidegrid Book | Retire as a module | “Bookings” works as navigation; Book reads as a verb and fragments the platform |
| Tidegrid Ops | Retire as a module | Tidegrid itself is the operations product |
| Tidegrid MusterDeck | Rename to **Tidegrid Deck** | “Muster” carries emergency and military associations; the compound needs explanation |
| Tidegrid Crew | Keep as **Crew** feature area | Clear, but not a separate product or budget |
| Tidegrid Gear | Keep as **Gear** feature area | Memorable and plain in context; no subbrand needed |
| Tidegrid Pay | Keep as **Payments** feature area | Avoid implying that Tidegrid, rather than Stripe and its partners, is the payment processor |
| Tidegrid Connect | Keep | A distinct external and technical surface can support a name |

### Naming rules for expansion

Create a new Tidegrid subname only when all four conditions hold:

1. Users enter through a distinct app, portal, or developer surface.
2. The capability has its own onboarding path.
3. Buyers or administrators can enable it as a coherent unit.
4. The name reduces explanation instead of adding maritime theater.

Use descriptive English before maritime language. Keep names to one word after Tidegrid. Avoid obscure seamanship terms, merged compounds, and words that imply regulatory approval or vessel control.

## 10. Three visual identity directions

The Tideline Grid mark, wordmark, palette, Sora display type, and Inter interface type are selected inputs. The three directions below define how that identity behaves across marketing and product surfaces. They do not replace the logo.

### Direction A: Operational Control

**Recommendation: adopt as the master expression.**

- **Creative concept:** A changing operating day held inside a clear structure.
- **Visual metaphor:** Tide bands cross a fixed grid; resources move while the operating frame stays stable.
- **Logo concept:** Use the current horizontal mark and wordmark with generous clear space. Let the symbol anchor grids, section dividers, and product states without turning it into a background pattern.
- **Symbol concept:** Vertical lines represent time and resource structure. Horizontal bands represent changing conditions and movement. Their crossing suggests coordination.
- **Typography:** Sora SemiBold or Bold for headlines and section labels. Inter Regular or Medium for body, data, and controls. Use tabular numerals for times, capacity, and money.
- **Color strategy:** Deep Forest and Foam form the primary contrast. Tide Lime marks action, selection, and positive readiness on dark surfaces. Functional status colors remain separate from the brand palette.
- **Iconography:** Two-pixel outlined icons with square geometry, rounded internal corners, and literal objects or actions. Use labels with every operational icon.
- **Photography:** Documentary images of real operators preparing, boarding, checking gear, and closing trips. Show working environments and mixed vessel types. Avoid empty seascapes as primary images.
- **Illustration:** Structured departure diagrams, manifest rows, capacity cells, and resource lines. Avoid decorative waves.
- **Interface:** Dark command surfaces for active operating views, light surfaces for setup and reporting. Cards expose time, vessel, trip type, capacity, and readiness without opening the record.
- **Motion:** Use 120 to 200 millisecond state changes. Move bands or rows along one axis to show assignment, queue, or progression. Honor reduced-motion settings.
- **Strengths:** Closest match to departure control; works in bright, high-pressure contexts; differentiates from travel photography and generic booking gradients.
- **Risks:** Dense use of dark green can feel heavy. The grid can become decorative noise. Tide Lime cannot carry small text on white.
- **Competitive distinction:** Presents Tidegrid as operating software first, while marine competitors tend to lead with booking widgets, boats, water, or price.

### Direction B: Working Waterfront

- **Creative concept:** Capable people running real departures.
- **Visual metaphor:** The selected grid acts as a work board that organizes crew, passengers, and equipment.
- **Logo concept:** Keep the current lockup. Use the mark in Deep Forest on Foam and reserve the dark application tile for field surfaces.
- **Symbol concept:** Treat the mark as a credential stamp on manifests, apparel, onboarding, and field documentation.
- **Typography:** Keep Sora and Inter. Use fewer oversized display headlines and more editorial captions, labels, and operator quotations.
- **Color strategy:** Lead with Foam and Mist; use Deep Forest for text and structure. Tide Lime appears in rules, checkmarks, and calls to action. Add warm neutral photography through wood, skin, metal, and dock materials without adding a permanent brown brand token.
- **Iconography:** The same system as Direction A, paired with role labels and process photography.
- **Photography:** Captains checking a manifest, deckhands staging gear, front-desk staff handling changes, and guests boarding. Favor available light and real locations.
- **Illustration:** Annotated process photography and simple readiness overlays.
- **Interface:** Light default surfaces with a high-contrast “Deck mode” for outdoor and offline use.
- **Motion:** Crossfades and short row transitions. Avoid continuous decorative motion.
- **Strengths:** Makes a technical product approachable to owner-operators and daily users; gives the brand human proof.
- **Risks:** Stock photography would turn the system into generic tourism marketing. A light interface needs field testing in glare.
- **Competitive distinction:** Shows the labor behind a departure instead of consumer experiences or aspirational boating.

### Direction C: Fleet Grid

- **Creative concept:** One operating standard across vessels and locations.
- **Visual metaphor:** Multiple departure grids share a common coordinate system.
- **Logo concept:** Keep the current lockup. Use the symbol as a compact fleet or location index beside vessel and location codes.
- **Symbol concept:** Extend the mark into data lines, map-free route traces, and readiness matrices. Do not turn it into a location pin or navigation chart.
- **Typography:** Keep Sora and Inter, with compact data tables, tabular numerals, and a strict type scale.
- **Color strategy:** Deep Forest and Harbor dominate. Mist separates data groups. Tide Lime marks selected scope and ready states on dark surfaces.
- **Iconography:** System and resource icons with location, vessel, and role badges.
- **Photography:** Limited use. Favor fleet overviews, operations rooms, maintenance preparation, and location context over lifestyle images.
- **Illustration:** Fleet matrices, dependency lines, readiness heatmaps, and capacity intersections.
- **Interface:** Higher information density with saved views, cross-location filters, exception queues, and audit history.
- **Motion:** Rows consolidate, filters narrow, and dependencies resolve. Keep motion functional and under 200 milliseconds.
- **Strengths:** Signals scale, discipline, and cross-location control.
- **Risks:** Can make Tidegrid feel designed for large fleets and can drift toward logistics or freight software.
- **Competitive distinction:** Creates an enterprise-capable expression without blue corporate software cues or commercial-shipping imagery.

### Interface brand principles

#### Outdoor and touch use

- Use a 16-pixel minimum body size and an 18-pixel minimum for field-critical instructions.
- Set primary field actions to at least 48 by 48 CSS pixels with 8 pixels between targets.
- Support full workflows without hover, drag, or multi-finger gestures.
- Keep critical actions visible without horizontal scrolling.
- Offer a high-contrast field mode and test it in direct sun on target phones and tablets.
- Require confirmation for irreversible actions, but keep routine boarding and checklist actions to one tap.

#### Information density

- Show exceptions before completed work.
- Keep one primary action per card or panel.
- Let operators expand detail without leaving the departure context.
- Preserve time, vessel, capacity, and readiness as fixed anchors.
- Use tabular numerals for times, counts, currency, and capacity.

#### Departure cards

Each card must show departure time, offering, vessel, booked capacity, readiness state, and the most urgent exception. Add crew or gear only when they require action. Do not use a rainbow of border colors as the primary state signal.

#### Manifest views

Keep passenger name, party, boarded state, payment, waiver, and role-specific requirement in stable columns. Support large-row field mode, rapid search, group boarding, undo, and a clear total boarded count. Put legal or operator-required manifest fields behind a named configuration, not an assumed universal standard.

#### Resource conflicts

Separate hard conflicts from warnings. A hard conflict names the resource, both assignments, overlap, and actions to resolve it. A warning names the rule that triggered it and lets an authorized user approve an exception with a reason.

#### Warnings

Use this grammar: **[Object] + [state] + [consequence] + [action].** Example: “Captain credential expired July 10. This departure is blocked. Assign an eligible captain.”

#### Weather disruptions

Show source, observation time, operator decision owner, decision deadline, affected departures, and customer action. Tidegrid can present weather data and workflow state; the captain or authorized operator makes the operating decision. Never auto-cancel solely from a weather feed.

#### Offline state

Display connection state, last successful sync time, number of queued changes, and records available offline. Confirm each local save. When the device reconnects, explain any conflict before overwriting another user’s change.

#### Payment and waiver state

Keep commercial payment state separate from platform-fee state, payout state, waiver state, and trip-card balance. Use “Paid,” “Balance due,” “Refund pending,” “Waiver complete,” and “Waiver missing.” Avoid one generic “Complete” label.

#### Crew and vessel readiness

Roll up readiness from named requirements, but keep the contributing checks visible. “Vessel ready” means configured Tidegrid checks have passed. It does not certify seaworthiness or regulatory compliance.

- I would trust this product with tomorrow’s passenger and departure records.
- This product appears to understand passenger-vessel operations.
- This product appears suitable for an operation my size.
- The promise feels credible for the capabilities shown.
- The visual system feels calm and clear under time pressure.
- The product appears useful when connectivity is limited.

Ask which claim needs proof before they would book a demo.

### Competitive differentiation questions

- How is this different from your current booking system?
- Which task appears to sit outside a normal booking platform?
- Which claimed difference feels generic?
- Would you add Tidegrid beside your current system or replace something? What?
- Which competitor would you compare it with first?
- Which one workflow would justify the switching effort?

### Visual identity tests

- Show the symbol at 16, 32, 64, and 128 pixels.
- Test dark, light, one-color, printed manifest, embroidered patch, and phone-in-sunlight mockups.
- Ask what the crossing lines represent before explaining them.
- Track “bars,” “jail,” “fence,” “spreadsheet,” “waves,” “tides,” and “grid” associations.
- Run task-based contrast and status tests, not preference voting.
- Ask participants to identify Ready, Warning, Blocked, Boarding, Pending, and Offline without relying on color.

### Tagline test

Rotate these three lines:

1. Every departure, under control.
2. From booked to back ashore.
3. One system for every departure.

Ask:

- What does the line promise?
- Which product would you expect behind it?
- Does it imply control over weather, navigation, safety, or compliance?
- Which line would you remember tomorrow?
- Which line would make you seek proof?

Use the recommended tagline only if fewer than 15% of participants interpret “control” as a weather, navigation, or safety guarantee after seeing the category context.

### Scoring framework

| Measure | Weight | Scoring method |
|---|---:|---|
| Correct product-category inference | 15 | Exact or close unprompted description |
| Correct target-customer inference | 10 | Passenger-vessel operator named without prompting |
| Memorability | 10 | Name and main job recalled after 20 minutes |
| Pronunciation | 5 | Name pronounced as intended |
| Spelling | 5 | Name typed correctly after hearing it |
| Trust | 15 | Five-point scale plus reason |
| Differentiation | 15 | Participant can state a non-generic difference |
| Operational credibility | 15 | Five-point scale after product application |
| Expansion potential | 5 | Fit for one-boat and fleet contexts |
| Negative associations | 5 | Reverse score for tide app, freight, bars, ERP, or safety overclaim |
| **Total** | **100** | Weighted score |

### Decision criteria

Advance the brand application only when all criteria pass:

- Counsel clears the name and proposed classes. This gate sits outside the research score.
- Weighted score reaches 80 out of 100 or better.
- At least 75% identify passenger-vessel or boat-operator software before prompting.
- At least 80% identify an owner, manager, dispatcher, captain, or related operator role as the user.
- At least 90% pronounce the name as intended and 80% spell it correctly after hearing it.
- Mean trust and operational-credibility scores each reach 4.0 out of 5.
- At least 70% can describe the departure-control difference without repeating supplied copy.
- No more than 20% infer a tide-prediction or weather-only product after the full lockup.
- No more than 15% read the symbol as bars, a fence, or another strong negative association.
- At least 70% of one-boat participants and 70% of fleet participants see a fit for their size.
- Task tests reach 95% correct status identification without color-only cues.

If the name fails legal clearance, retain category, positioning, research script, messaging hierarchy, and experience principles. Retest the replacement name and revised wordmark with eight fresh participants before production.

### Research output

Produce a coded transcript set, raw score sheet, segment comparison, issue severity log, recommendation memo, and a decision record. Preserve direct participant language for copy development, with consent and anonymization.

## 17. Final concise brand brief for a design agency

### Project

Create the production identity and application system for a working-name B2B SaaS platform that coordinates passenger-vessel departures. The team has selected the Tideline Grid direction shown in [TideGrid Logo and Theme.png](<TideGrid Logo and Theme.png>). Begin production only after written name clearance.

### Business objective

Help passenger-vessel operators understand within five seconds that the product runs the operational work around each departure, beyond checkout and calendar scheduling. Build credibility with owner-operators and multi-location fleets without drifting into freight, marina, weather, or consumer-travel cues.

### Audience

Economic buyers are charter and dive-shop owners, general managers, fleet operators, and operations managers. Daily users include dispatchers, front-desk staff, captains, divemasters, guides, deckhands, accountants, and passengers.

### Position

- **Category:** Passenger-vessel operations platform
- **Positioning:** Tidegrid keeps each departure crewed, equipped, documented, boarded, completed, and reconciled in one connected record.
- **Promise:** Every departure, under control.
- **Homepage lead:** Run every departure from booking to back ashore.

### Personality

Prepared, clear, calm, decisive, knowledgeable, practical, and trustworthy. The reference is an excellent captain or operations manager. Avoid cute, pirate, luxury-yacht, militaristic, bureaucratic, futuristic, and generic Silicon Valley cues.

### Selected creative system

- Preserve the vertical grid crossed by three tide bands.
- Preserve Deep Forest, Pine Green, Tide Lime, Mist, Foam, and Harbor as the core palette.
- Use Sora for display and Inter for interface and body copy.
- Build Operational Control as the master expression.
- Add documentary working-waterfront photography and denser fleet applications where the audience requires them.
- Keep important status separate from brand color and pair color with icons and labels.

### Required applications

- Horizontal wordmark, symbol, app icon, and favicon
- Dark, light, one-color, reverse, print, and embroidery versions
- Homepage hero and departure-lifecycle diagram
- Departure board, card, manifest, readiness, disruption, offline, and closeout screens
- Sales sheet, trade-show wall, staff apparel, and printed manifest
- One-boat, dive, fishing, cruise, private-charter, and fleet examples

### Product-experience constraints

- Field-critical targets at least 48 by 48 CSS pixels
- Body text at least 16 pixels; field-critical text at least 18 pixels
- No status communicated by color alone
- Tide Lime never used as small text on white or Foam
- Connection, last-sync, queued-change, and conflict state remain visible
- Captain authority and product limits remain explicit in weather and readiness workflows

### Creative constraints

Avoid wheels, anchors, cartoon boats, waves under the wordmark, compass roses, captain hats, rope, location pins, sunset gradients, yacht lifestyle photography, and decorative nautical props. Use resource lines, tide marks, manifest structure, departure grids, time and capacity intersections, and real operating work.

### Production deliverables

Provide SVG, PDF, EPS if required by vendors, CMYK and RGB masters, transparent PNGs, app-store sizes, favicon set, design tokens, type specifications, icon grid, photography guidance, motion rules, accessibility documentation, templates, and a usage guide with clear-space and minimum-size rules.

### Proof and validation

Test the symbol at 16 pixels, in direct sun, on office and field screens, on a laser-printed manifest, in one color, and as embroidery. Validate category comprehension, target-customer inference, trust, operational credibility, and status recognition with passenger-vessel operators. Treat name clearance as a separate gate.

### Success

An operator should identify passenger-vessel operations, understand the departure-control difference, trust the interface under time pressure, and recognize the mark without seeing a boat or anchor.
